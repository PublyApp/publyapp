## Findings

1. **Major — R5-1/O18: a policy fingerprint is not a schedule-epoch fence; the deliberate `A → B → A` reuse admits stale fires from the first A epoch.** §4.3 [L965](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:965), [L985](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:985), [L1012](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1012); §5.3 [L1882](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1882), [L1890](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1890); O18 [L3071](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:3071). The exact-match mechanism correctly rejects an ordinary A→B straggler, but it intentionally treats a first-epoch A trigger as current after B→A because the hash repeats. That is unsafe for an already-acquired/delayed Quartz execution: a fire scheduled during the old A epoch—or during a disabled interval—can execute after the definition returns to identical A/enabled state and pass both the fingerprint and current-enabled checks. The same ABA exists for delete/recreate of the same `job_key` and policy. The statement that a monotonic revision “would get [A→B→A] wrong” confuses policy equality with registration-epoch identity: the new A trigger should be accepted, but the old A execution should not. **Fix:** add a distinct schedule epoch (`schedule_revision` or random `schedule_epoch`) changed on every cron/timezone/gap-policy revision, disable→enable, and active-definition recreation; stamp it into the trigger and require an exact epoch match. Keep the fingerprint for policy-change detection. An alternative is an immutable `schedule_effective_from` fence checked against `scheduled_fire_at`, but it must remain separate from the advancing reconciliation watermark. Extend the forced-fire spec to A→B→A, disable→enable, and delete/recreate delayed old executions.

2. **Major — R5-2/O19: the materialized expiry boundary is neither the sweep’s boundary nor the requeue gate.** §4.2 [L775](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:775), [L789](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:789), [L819](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:819); §4.5 [L1273](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1273), [L1282](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1282); O7 [L2991](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:2991). Dead-lettering materializes `external_state_expires_at`, and the prose calls it the authoritative/enforced boundary, but the sweep ignores it and recomputes `prepared_at + current EMAIL_PREPARED_SEND_RETENTION_DAYS`; requeue branches only on stored status and never checks database `now()` against `external_state_expires_at`. Therefore a row remains `Present` and requeueable after its cutoff until the asynchronous sweep happens. Changing the env var makes the durable timestamp disagree with actual deletion: shortening can delete before the recorded expiry; lengthening can retain/requeue after it. This violates “at most seven days,” makes the dashboard timestamp dishonest, and leaves O7’s “override without a design change” semantics undefined for existing DLQs. **Fix:** choose and state prospective versus retroactive config semantics. For prospective semantics, make `external_state_expires_at` authoritative: sweep on `d.external_state_expires_at <= now()` and make requeue require `now() < external_state_expires_at` under lock; an expired requeue attempt should atomically run the same delete+Expired+audit transition before returning `PreparedStateExpired`. For retroactive overrides, specify a conditioned migration/recalculation of outstanding `Present` rows before the new window takes effect. Add pre-sweep-after-cutoff and retention-config-change specs.

3. **Major — R5-2/O19: requeue and expiry have a lock-order inversion, and the Missing branch cannot both persist its anomaly and roll back the requeue as specified.** §4.2 [L735](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:735), [L738](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:738), [L762](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:762), [L781](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:781), [L807](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:807); §4.5 [L1276](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1276), [L1286](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1286), [L1312](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1312). Requeue’s stated sequence locks/updates the DLQ row for the single-use guard and then locks `email_prepared_sends`; the sweep locks the prepared row (`FOR UPDATE OF p`) and then updates the DLQ row. A requeue holding DLQ and waiting on prepared can deadlock with a sweep holding prepared and waiting on DLQ; `SKIP LOCKED` does not help once each transaction already owns the opposite resource. Separately, the `Present`-but-absent branch says to stamp `Missing` + audit, reject, and roll back the requeue, while the operation is declared one transaction and the text says rejection rolls back the DLQ stamp. A rollback also erases `Missing` and its audit, contradicting the durable anomaly promise. **Fix:** define one lock order across both paths—prefer DLQ first, then prepared. Have the sweep select/lock eligible DLQ rows first (`FOR UPDATE OF d SKIP LOCKED`) before deleting their prepared rows. In requeue, validate external state before writing the single-use stamp/new job. If `Present` has no row, conditionally write `Missing` + audit in a transaction that commits, return the domain rejection after commit, and never create/stamp a requeue. Add a barrier-controlled sweep∥requeue spec and a fresh-context assertion that a rejected Missing detection remains persisted/audited.

4. **Major — R5-2/O19: `NeverPrepared` is inferred from absence, so the six-value model is not total, and its named malformed-payload path cannot satisfy the mandatory email terminal hook.** §4.2 [L685](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:685), [L696](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:696), [L711](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:711); §5.1 [L1756](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:1756); §5.4 [L2125](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:2125); §9 [L2622](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:2622), [L2655](/home/radan/Projects/PublyApp/jobs-design/docs/implementation-plans/jobs-worker-infrastructure.md:2655). At dead-letter time, “prepared row absent” is classified as `NeverPrepared`. That cannot distinguish true pre-PREPARE failure from prepared state lost/corrupted before dead-lettering—the latter is exactly the anomaly `Missing` claims to represent, but `Missing` is reachable only after a DLQ row was first stamped `Present`. In the worst case, an ambiguous provider attempt followed by premature scratch loss is labeled `NeverPrepared` and requeue is allowed with new bytes/key. The concrete `malformed payload` example adds a second unimplemented edge: the engine says every terminal path invokes `OnTerminalFailureAsync`, and the email hook says it writes `email_log(PermanentlyFailed)`, but malformed/missing IDs cannot reload a recipient while `email_log.recipient` is `NOT NULL`; a throwing hook rolls back terminal settlement forever. **Fix:** either narrow `Missing` explicitly to post-dead-letter loss and stop claiming total loss/corruption distinction, or persist a minimal non-sensitive “PREPARE occurred” marker outside the deletable scratch (for example on `job_queue`, copied to DLQ) so absence can be classified honestly. Separately define invalid-before-handler settlement: DLQ-only with `NeverPrepared` and no email-log row, or a schema-supported `recipient unavailable` outcome that does not invent an address. Add pre-DLQ scratch-loss and malformed-email-payload terminal-settlement specs. Phase-3 alerting should sample durable `external_state_status = Missing` rows, not rely only on a one-shot in-process counter that can be lost between the status commit and metric emission.

The Round-1 history still says prepared bytes are `text` and global gauges are leader-gated; later rounds supersede both with `bytea` and per-replica sampling, but the historical bullets are not labeled as intermediate-state descriptions. **Fix:** change the vocabulary to O6–O19 (noting the absent O8 ordering is intentional), and prefix historical round summaries with “state after that round; later entries may supersede” or annotate the affected bullets with their superseding round ids.

## Author-decision and scope rulings

- **O18:** rejected as written. A fingerprint correctly answers “is the policy text identical?” but cannot answer “did this execution come from the current registration epoch?” A→B→A should accept the newly registered A trigger, not a delayed execution acquired under the first A.
- **O19:** accepted in direction, not yet in mechanism. The four metadata columns contain only status/timestamps—no recipient, body, token, hash, or provider key—so retaining them for 90 days does not reopen O16’s sensitive-byte exposure. The defects above concern transition completeness and concurrency, not the privacy rationale.
- **O7 extension / index scope:** keep it. Every named global-age sweep needs an index beginning with its age predicate. Adding the three sibling indexes while documenting the shared idiom prevents the exact same scan defect from surviving in adjacent tables.

## Attacked and held

- The ordinary A→B fence works: every dynamic trigger carries the fingerprint, an old fingerprint is rejected under the definition lock, trigger replacement is required, and the two occurrence-writing paths share definition-first order. The remaining defect is specifically epoch reuse/ABA, not the basic fence or the occurrence-lock proof.
- In the uncontended happy path, the prepared-send CTE’s `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM purged …)` genuinely prevents an `Expired` stamp unless that statement deleted the matching bytes; an audit-insert failure rolls the entire statement back.
- The six status values have useful intended meanings after a DLQ row exists: `Transferred` prevents false Missing on ancestors, `Expired` survives byte deletion, and `Missing` is distinct from policy expiry. The four columns are non-sensitive metadata.
- R5-3 is complete. The 30-day alert-lease window is safely above the 60-second live lease, the index matches the global predicate, and the extra sibling indexes are necessary.
- Active §8/§9/§10/§11 references match the renumbered headings; no live two-lane, in-process-signal, global registry-bypass, or leader-gated sampler contract remains outside clearly historical text.
- The accepted code-alignment markers are accurate against the branch tips: 634 still stamps only `job_key`, uses default `WithCronSchedule`, and lacks the new definition fields/locks; 633 lacks `external_state_*`; 809 still carries the earlier prepared-envelope shape. These are disclosed doc-right/code-lags, not newly concealed mismatches.
- C8 remains accepted: immediate cancellation, `CancellationToken.None` settlement/release, fencing, and idempotent replay form a coherent shutdown contract for this system.
